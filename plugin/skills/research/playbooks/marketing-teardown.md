# Marketing teardown

How does this company get its customers? Find the one to three channels that carry it, and connect them. Think like an
operator copying the playbook, not a describer listing what exists: "They buy installs with UGC ads that all open on
the same hook, then convert in a three-screen paywall" beats "They are active on TikTok".

## Where to look (each is a Deep subagent)

- **Site and funnel**: the homepage promise, pricing page, signup or paywall steps, free trial terms, sitemap and
  robots.txt (which pages they want found), llms.txt.
- **Search**: what they rank for (their blog, comparison and "alternatives" pages, programmatic pages), whether AI
  answers cite them.
- **Paid**: Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency Center, LinkedIn's ad library, TikTok's Creative Center: how many
  ads, how long the oldest has run (long-running ads are the winners), the hooks they repeat.
- **Short-form and social**: their own accounts and creators posting about them: posting rate, the formats that get
  views, whether it's paid UGC.
- **Founder-led**: the founders' own posts, podcasts and talks.
- **Community**: Reddit, Hacker News, Product Hunt, Discord, forums: launches, who talks about them and what they say.
- **Apps**: App Store and Google Play pages: rank, ratings, screenshots' promise, in-app purchase prices.
- **History**: the Wayback Machine for how the homepage promise and pricing changed.

## On the page

- Living **Verdict**: one line: the engine and why it works.
- Living **Channels** table: channel · active / likely / dormant / unknown · strength 0-3 · the signal, with `[E#]`.
- Rounds go deeper on one channel at a time.
- Living **Steal this**: three to five concrete moves the user could copy this week, each citing evidence.
- Unknowns with how to check by hand (an ad library link, a search to run). Never "they don't run ads" from a source
  you couldn't open.
- Never invent spend, revenue or install numbers. Counts from a sample say "in the sample".
